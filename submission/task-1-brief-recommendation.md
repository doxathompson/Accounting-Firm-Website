# Website Recommendation – London Accounting Firm

## 1. Proposed Sitemap
I would propose a deliberately simple five-page website:
- Home 
- Services 
- About / Team 
- Resources: For Sole Traders & Limited Companies 
- Contact 

## 2. Homepage Priorities
The homepage should prioritise three things immediately:
1. **Target Audience Identification**: It should clearly state who the firm serves: sole traders and limited companies in London with turnover under £500k. This helps the right visitor self-identify quickly.
2. **Clear Value Proposition**: It should explain the firm’s difference: reliable, responsive, personal accounting support from a small team. The message should make the visitor feel they can speak to an actual person who will explain things clearly and respond when needed.
3. **Obvious Next Step**: The homepage should provide one obvious next step for visitors.

## 3. Core Call to Action
The core call to action should be **Book a free consultation**. 

This frames the next step as a conversation rather than a price comparison. Small business owners looking for an accountant are often concerned about being ignored, confused, or treated like a number. The website should reassure them that this firm understands small businesses and offers direct, responsive support. I would avoid "Get a quote" language, because those choices would make the firm feel price-led and transactional.

## 4. Reasoning Summary
The client is a small three-person accounting firm based in London, serving sole traders and limited companies with turnover under £500k. Since the firm does not compete on price, the website should avoid looking cheap, transactional, or overly corporate. Pricing tables and “starting from” fees should be completely avoided.

The site should remain tight because a large, overbuilt website would make the firm appear bigger and less personal than it is. A three-person accounting practice gains more trust by showing accessibility, clarity, and real human support rather than trying to look like a large corporate firm. Overall, the website should feel clean, professional, approachable, and human. Its purpose is to make a visitor think: “This firm understands businesses like mine, and I can actually speak to a real person.”
